Little Mayor gives you 40 days and two cards a day. The town that comes out
at the end depends far more on planning than on luck, because everyone gets
the same cards on the same day. These are the rules the game uses, and the
habits that turn a 50-person village into a 150-person city.

## How the score works

Your score is the number of people in your town when day 40 ends. Only
**houses** have people. Everything else is there to make houses bigger or to
pay for them.

A house only counts when it is **linked**: it has to touch a road (side by
side, not corner to corner) that leads back to the Town Hall. An unlinked
house shows a red warning sign and has nobody living in it.

A linked house has 2 people, plus:

| Next to the house | People |
| --- | --- |
| A park in one of the 8 squares around it | +2 |
| A school up to 2 squares away | +3 |
| A clinic up to 2 squares away | +3 |
| Each shop in the 8 squares around it (up to 2) | +1 |

So the best house in the game has 12 people: park, school, clinic and two
shops. You'll see it as an orange-roofed apartment block.

## Money

Your town earns coins at the end of every day:

- 2 coins from the Town Hall.
- From each house, its people plus 2, divided by 4 and rounded down. A
  2-person cottage pays 1 coin, a 12-person block pays 3.
- From each shop, 1 coin for every linked house in the 8 squares around it.

Prices go up by half for every 20 people in your town ("LAND X1.5" and so
on at the top of the screen). Building on a square that's already used
knocks down what was there for 2 extra coins.

## Tips

1. **Plan the roads first.** Roads don't use up a day, only coins. The four
   short roads round the Town Hall fill up fast, and a house built at the
   end of a road can block it for good. Keep at least two road ends free.
2. **Loops beat dead ends.** A ring road one square in from the edge meets
   the Town Hall's roads and leaves almost every square next to a road.
3. **Put schools and clinics in the middle of houses.** Their reach is two
   squares in every direction, so a well-placed school touches up to 24
   squares. One in a corner wastes most of that.
4. **Parks only help the 8 squares around them.** Put each park where it
   touches as many houses as possible.
5. **Build shops early.** Each shop is +1 person to its neighbours and a
   small daily income that adds up over 40 days, before land gets dear.
6. **Discarding is fine.** A school with nowhere useful to go is worth less
   than a free day. Look at the **NEXT** card before you decide.
7. **Spend your coins late.** Coins aren't part of the score. In the last
   ten days, knock down weak cottages and rebuild the squares that would
   gain the most.

## Medals

Bronze at 50 people, silver at 90, gold at 125 and the Key to the City at
150. Our test bot, which plays every card greedily, usually finishes between
120 and 145, so the Key to the City takes real planning.
